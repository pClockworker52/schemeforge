The big picture

Arena 1 is closed and published. This page is about the second arena: a judge for third-medium contact, what it can already do, and what it is for.

Short answer

The Arena 2 judge exists and is exact where it can be. Four of its seven steps are done; the next step decides whether there is anything worth searching for.

The judge runs a candidate contact medium through gap closure, shear, a patch test and Hertz contact, and compares it with exact solutions and with a classical contact reference. Where the answer is exact, the judge reproduces it to round-off; for Hertz its own error floor is about 1 % on the dev mesh. The published medium already shows the trade-off the search will attack.

Next: the first real held-out run (on Peter's PC, running now), then the zoo of published media and the trade-off front: if the zoo shows no trade-off, Arena 2 stops there.

2. What Arena 2 is about

When two bodies touch in a finite-element model, the contact is usually enforced with special constraints that are hard to make robust. Third-medium contact instead fills the gap with a fictitious, very soft material. As the gap closes, the medium is squeezed and stiffens, and it carries the contact force. It is simple and works inside any standard solver, which is why it is used in topology optimisation.

Its weakness is a trade-off in the medium's stiffness γ. A stiffer medium transmits force through the open gap before contact (leak) and leaves a residual layer after it; a softer medium behaves more like ideal contact but makes Newton's method struggle. The search candidate is the medium's energy ψ(C): a few lines of jax.numpy. The question for Phase 7 is whether an energy exists that does better on both sides.

C2 HertzAn elastic cylinder pressed onto a flat through the medium: contact width, peak pressure, profile, leakClassical contact within 1 % of the Hertz formula (dev mesh), 0.3 % on fine meshes

Around these: hard constraints that reject a broken medium before any case runs (non-finite, negative energy, no resistance to compression, a hidden absolute constant), held-out families generated from the secret seed, and a promo suite that also measures mesh sensitivity and the largest load step that still converges. The dev suite runs in under 30 s per candidate.

The trade-off is real

Softer media leak less and close better, and they cost Newton steps until they fail. That is the precondition for a search, already visible with one zoo member.

The judge has its own trade-off

Until the regularization slot exists, the judge adds a fixed Hessian regularization. Too strong adds a fake 4 % tension outside the contact; too weak and Newton fails on fine meshes. The chosen value (0.3 % artifact) is documented and can be revisited.

Not every metric is trustworthy yet

Contact width and peak pressure are stable across meshes (within 0.4 points). The profile error and the leak outside contact move with the mesh, so targets should rest mainly on the first two.

5. What comes next

  • Now (WSL): the first real held-out run of the zoo, with the secret seed.
  • Step 5, the decisive one: the published media (Wriggers 2013, Bluhm 2021, the FElupe variants, FElupe's mixed formulation) and classical penalty contact, each over a sweep of γ, plotted as a front. If the front shows no trade-off, Arena 2 stops with a short report, before any search money is spent.
  • Step 6: blind audit of the references by an independent agent, Target D pre-registered from the front, judge tagged contact-v1.0.
  • Step 7: the engine and agents set up for this arena, a calibration run that must rediscover known media, and a preview of the agents' hunches with the closest published relative named for each.
  • Then Gate 6 (Peter), and Phase 7: the actual search for a better medium.

What to hope for. The field is young (published mainly since 2013), the medium's energy has few established forms, and the trade-off is practically painful. That gives the agents more room than Arena 1's forty-year-old limiters, and natural sources for transfer: foams, granular media and tissue all stiffen under compression in their own ways. Whether a hunch turns out new is decided only by the referee after a literature check.
