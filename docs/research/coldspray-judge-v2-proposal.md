# Cold spray: scoring rule v2, a proposal (draft, not adopted)

Drafted 2026-11-07 in an autonomous block while Peter was away. **Nothing here is in force.** Adopting it is
Peter's decision, and it would apply only to predictions frozen after adoption. No recorded verdict changes.
Script: `scripts/judge_v2_sensitivity.py`.

## The problem

1. **Windows narrower than the measurement error.** The primary HG2018b (read 2026-11-07, rows P18) prints windows
   of 5–29 m/s: Zn 518–523, Ni 650–660, Al 797–824, Cu 539–568. Their velocity error is ±2 %, about ±10–16 m/s per
   shot. A window runs from the highest rebound to the lowest adhesion. Each edge is one shot, so each edge is
   uncertain by about ±2 %. Rule S1 scores a prediction 1 m/s outside such a window as a miss, although the miss is
   inside the measurement error.
2. **Quotes and re-analyses scored instead of primaries.** v1 scored the H20 rows (Hassani2020's equal-count
   windows). For Cu and Zn these do not even contain the primary window. For Au, Hassani2020's window does not
   contain the primary HG2019b value (caveat 1 of the v1 results).
3. **Inconsistent treatment already.** Rule B6 (2026-11-04) widens nothing. It builds a window as v ± the stated
   error only when no bounds are printed. So a paper that prints a narrow bracket is scored more harshly than one
   that prints only a midpoint.

## Proposed rules (draft)

- **R1. Primaries first.** Score against the primary source's window once it has been read. Rows quoting it, or
  re-analysing the same impacts under another definition, are kept in the table but not scored (as Q rows are under
  S3).
- **R2. Widen brackets by the stated velocity error.** A window printed as a bracket (equal-count window, or
  highest rebound to lowest adhesion) is scored as [lo (1 − e), hi (1 + e)], where e is the paper's stated relative
  velocity error. Windows printed as value ± uncertainty, and B6 windows, are not widened again.
- **R3. No stated error, no widening.** The row is scored as printed and flagged. This is the case for DW and PA
  in our extraction.
- **R4. The same windows feed the verdict rules.** Typical window width w = median width of the scored windows, as
  in the frozen rules. So w grows with R2.

## What the sensitivity says

1. **Five of the eight verdicts are robust.** CS-01 (inconclusive), CS-02, CS-03, CS-06 (refuted), CS-08, CS-16 and
   CS-07 on B14 do not move under either variant.
2. **R1 alone is harsh, R1 and R2 together are not.** With primaries but no widening, CS-05 crosses its kill line
   (Al d = 0.104). With the velocity error added, it returns to inconclusive (0.084). The narrow primary window,
   not the size law, made the difference.
3. **R2 decides two B10 verdicts that are undetermined today.** CS-07 on B10 becomes inconclusive, and CS-09
   becomes refuted: the five B10 values spread 77 m/s, more than 2w = 70 m/s. B6 was explicitly not applied to B10
   when it was adopted, so these stay undetermined unless Peter decides otherwise.
4. **The oxide-chemistry signal weakens.** RH-95-D against RH-95 (−68.5 m/s) is 1.6 window widths as scored, but
   only 0.9 widths with the velocity error. That bears on whether an oxide-chemistry hunch would discriminate on
   anything (HANDOFF, open item 5).

## Open points for Peter

- **Adopt R1–R4 for future freezes?** The recommendation is yes for R1 and R2. Without R2, R1 penalises misses
  that lie inside the measurement error.
- **Is the ±2 % per shot random or a shared calibration error?** If it is shared within one campaign (same camera
  calibration), contrasts inside one paper (Δ in B10, B02 and B14) should use the unwidened w. Only absolute
  scores against a model should use the widened windows. The papers do not say which; R2 as drafted treats it as
  per shot.
- **B10 under B6:** leave CS-07 and CS-09 undetermined (as recorded), or rescore them under B6 as a recorded
  amendment?
